# Constitution of [Association name]

**DRAFT FOR REVIEW — NOT ADOPTED**  
Revised 7 September 2026 · England · Nonprofit unincorporated association

Complete the adoption record only after the proposed name, purposes and governance have been reviewed. This draft does not establish registered charity or CASC status. Supporting notes are in ADOPTION-NOTES.md and do not form part of the constitution.

## 1. Name, form and area

1.1 The name is **[Association name]** (the Association). The Association is a nonprofit unincorporated association governed by this constitution and the law of England and Wales.

1.2 The Association may operate its martial-arts programme under the working name **The Pit Combat Academy**. Its adopted legal name must identify the operator on contracts, bank accounts, funding applications and public organisational information.

1.3 Its principal area of benefit is Greater Manchester, particularly the Oldham and Rochdale area. The committee shall maintain a correspondence address and notify members of changes.

## 2. Purposes

2.1 The Association exists to provide accessible community sport, physical activity and opportunities for personal and social development, particularly for young people, in its area of benefit.

2.2 It will promote participation in martial arts and other amateur sport; support confidence, discipline, respect and positive community relationships through coaching and mentoring; and reduce financial and practical barriers to participation.

2.3 Activities may benefit adults as well as young people. The Association shall not operate for the private profit of its members, founders or committee.

2.4 It will pursue these purposes through structured coaching and training, opportunities to volunteer and develop skills, community outreach and cooperation with local schools and community organisations. Activities shall encourage safe participation, physical wellbeing and social connection; these objectives do not authorise clinical diagnosis or treatment.

## 3. Powers and use of resources

3.1 In furtherance of its purposes the Association may organise classes and events, engage suitably qualified coaches, employ staff and involve volunteers, acquire equipment, seek grants and donations, charge reasonable fees, collaborate with other organisations and obtain appropriate insurance.

3.2 Its money and property shall be used only for its purposes. No surplus or assets may be distributed to members, except permitted expenses or reasonable payment for actual goods or services under clause 8.

3.3 A lease, borrowing, guarantee, purchase of land or disposal of a substantial asset requires prior approval of a general meeting by a two-thirds majority of votes cast. The proposed transaction and its personal-liability implications must be described in the meeting notice. No individual is obliged to sign or guarantee a commitment without their express agreement. The Association has no separate legal personality; this constitution does not limit liability to third parties.

## 4. Membership

4.1 Membership is open to individuals who support the purposes and agree to this constitution and applicable conduct rules. Applications shall be considered fairly and without unlawful discrimination. Capacity limits shall use an objective published waiting-list process.

4.2 Adults aged 18 or over are voting members. Each has one vote after 30 days of membership; founding members admitted at the adoption meeting may vote immediately. Junior members under 18 have no vote and shall be given suitable opportunities to express their views. A parent does not automatically acquire membership or a vote through a child; a parent may apply in their own right. Purchasing a trial class alone does not constitute membership.

4.3 The committee shall set and publish subscriptions, class charges and an affordability policy, including a confidential route to request concessions. A pending hardship request shall not of itself suspend voting rights. Membership decisions must not be based on ability to make donations.

4.4 The secretary shall keep the membership register and record admissions, resignations and the applicable membership category. Membership continues until resignation, death or termination under this clause; it does not expire automatically after a fixed term. Members may resign in writing. The committee may end membership for subscriptions outstanding by more than 60 days only after written notice, a further 14-day opportunity to resolve the matter, and consideration of any hardship request.

4.5 Refusal of membership, suspension or expulsion requires a fair reason connected to safety, conduct, capacity or the purposes. Written reasons and an opportunity to respond shall be provided. Urgent protective suspension may take immediate effect, with reasons and review within 14 days. Appeals may be submitted within 21 days and shall be heard within 28 days by three impartial adults appointed by the committee who were not involved in the original decision; suitable external persons may be appointed. The appellant may bring a supporter. The panel shall give its reasoned decision in writing. This process does not replace safeguarding referrals or statutory rights.

## 5. General meetings

5.1 An annual general meeting (AGM) shall be held within 12 months of adoption and thereafter at intervals of no more than 15 months. It shall receive the annual activity report and accounts, elect the committee and consider notified business.

5.2 The committee may call a special general meeting. It must hold the meeting within 42 days of a written request stating the business from at least 20% of voting members, rounded up. If it fails to give notice within 14 days, those requesting may convene the meeting using the same notice and quorum rules.

5.3 At least 21 clear days' written notice, excluding the day sent and the meeting day, shall be given to all members, stating time, venue or online access, agenda and the full text of any constitutional amendment or dissolution proposal. Notices may be sent electronically to the contact details supplied by members. Meetings may be physical, online or hybrid if all participants can hear, contribute and vote.

5.4 The quorum is the greater of five voting members or 20% of all voting members, rounded up. Where there are fewer than five voting members, all must attend. Quorum must remain present during decisions. An inquorate meeting may discuss business but may not decide it; a reconvened meeting requires fresh notice and the same quorum.

5.5 The chair shall preside; if absent or conflicted the voting members present shall elect another chair for the relevant business. Ordinary resolutions pass by a simple majority of votes cast. Abstentions are excluded. A tied resolution fails; there is no casting vote. Proxy and postal voting are not permitted. Minutes shall record attendance, decisions, votes and declared conflicts.

## 6. Management committee and elections

6.1 The committee consists of five to seven adult voting members, including a chair, secretary and treasurer, each held by a different person. At least three committee members must be independent of paid coaches and suppliers: they must not be paid by the Association other than expenses, or be close family, household members or business partners of those receiving payment.

6.2 The AGM elects each officer separately and then the remaining members. Written nominations, with the candidate's consent, must reach the secretary seven days before the meeting. Each voting member may cast one vote per officer election and up to the number of vacancies for ordinary committee seats. The highest vote totals win; ties are resolved by a further ballot between tied candidates and, if still tied, a transparent draw overseen by the meeting. Seats must be filled consistently with clause 6.1; if that is impossible the vacancies remain and clause 6.5 applies.

6.3 Committee terms run until the next AGM; members may stand again. The founding meeting elects the initial committee using these rules, with nominations accepted at that meeting. Founders have no permanent seat, veto, ownership entitlement or special voting right.

6.4 A committee member may resign in writing and ceases to hold office on ceasing to be an adult member, becoming legally ineligible, or removal by a two-thirds vote of a general meeting after at least 21 clear days' notice of the proposed removal and a fair opportunity to respond. The committee may co-opt an eligible member to a vacancy until the next AGM, preserving clause 6.1.

6.5 If fewer than five committee members or fewer than three independent members remain, the remaining committee may only protect people and assets, meet existing obligations and arrange co-option or a general meeting within 28 days to fill vacancies. It may not approve new connected-person payments or major commitments.

## 7. Committee proceedings and duties

7.1 The committee shall meet at least quarterly. Members shall receive seven clear days' notice and an agenda; urgent meetings require at least 48 hours' notice. Meetings may be physical, online or hybrid.

7.2 The quorum is three unconflicted committee members and at least half of the serving committee, rounded up. Decisions pass by a simple majority of those present and entitled to vote. A tie fails. A conflict cannot be overridden to achieve a quorum. Minutes and a register of interests shall be maintained.

7.3 The committee is responsible for the budget, safe operations, staff and volunteer arrangements, compliance, policies and reporting to members. It may delegate written tasks and spending limits but retains oversight. It shall appoint a safeguarding lead and deputy, and provide a reporting route when a concern involves the usual lead.

7.4 The Association shall maintain and apply suitable safeguarding, safer recruitment, equality, complaints, health and safety and data-protection arrangements. Junior activities may begin only when required safeguarding arrangements, appropriately eligible checks, insurance, staffing and lawful safe premises are in place. Activity rules must reflect age, ability, coaching competence and relevant governing-body and insurer requirements.

7.5 The chair coordinates the committee's work, chairs meetings subject to clause 5.5 and represents the Association when authorised. The secretary arranges notices and agendas, keeps minutes and the membership register, and manages correspondence. The treasurer oversees the financial records, budget and reports required by clause 9. These roles do not confer a power to act alone outside delegated authority.

7.6 The committee may adopt reasonable operational rules consistent with this constitution and the law, and shall make them available to members. Rules cannot change membership voting rights, election arrangements or the constitution without the approval required by clause 10.

## 8. Conflicts, expenses and paid coaching

8.1 Committee service is voluntary. Reasonable evidenced expenses incurred for the purposes may be reimbursed, with approval by two unconflicted authorised persons other than the claimant.

8.2 A member must disclose an actual or potential conflict, including personal, family, household or business interests. They shall leave the discussion after any requested factual explanation, shall not vote, approve payment or count towards the quorum, and shall not influence the decision outside the meeting. These rules also apply to conflicted voting members at general meetings.

8.3 The Association may pay reasonable market-based amounts for necessary coaching or other actual goods or services, including from a founder, committee member or connected person, only where permitted by applicable law. It must not pay for holding committee office.

8.4 Before any connected-person arrangement is made, at least three independent, unconflicted committee members must approve it at a quorate meeting. They must record the need, alternatives and market comparisons, why the arrangement serves the Association's interests, its written scope, the amount or maximum amount payable and review date. All connected beneficiaries, including related founders, must be excluded under clause 8.2. The number of committee members benefiting directly or through connected persons must remain a minority of the full committee.

8.5 Every paid arrangement must be documented before work starts, reviewed at least annually, and properly classified for employment and tax purposes. Related-party payments and the approval process shall be disclosed in the annual accounts or accompanying report. If independent approval cannot be obtained, the arrangement shall not proceed. If charity law applies, any additional statutory conditions or Charity Commission authority must be satisfied before commitment; this clause does not override them.

## 9. Finance and records

9.1 The financial year ends on 31 March. The treasurer shall maintain accounts, supporting receipts and a record of restricted grants and donations, and report balances, actual spending and commitments to each committee meeting.

9.2 Funds shall be held in an account in the adopted Association name. At least three bank signatories shall be appointed. Every payment or new payment mandate requires documented approval by two authorised, unconflicted people, neither of whom is the recipient or connected to the recipient. The two approvers must not be close family, household members or business partners of each other. The banking service shall support dual authorisation. Blank signed cheques and shared banking credentials are prohibited.

9.3 The committee shall approve an annual budget. Spending outside it exceeding £500 for a single commitment, or linked commitments, requires a prior minuted committee decision. This does not remove the two-person approval requirement or the additional approvals in clauses 3 and 8. Cash collections shall be counted and recorded by two people and banked promptly.

9.4 Restricted funds shall be applied only in accordance with their terms. Donations shall be refused or returned where their conditions cannot lawfully be met. Tax relief, registered status and Gift Aid eligibility must not be represented without a proper basis.

9.5 Annual accounts shall be presented to the AGM and reviewed by a competent person independent of the committee and of the preparation of the accounts. Any audit, examination, filing or tax requirement imposed by law or a funder must also be met. Adult members may request reasonable access to accounts, general-meeting minutes and this constitution, subject to protection of personal and confidential information.

## 10. Amendments

10.1 This constitution may be amended by a two-thirds majority of votes cast at a quorate general meeting called with the notice and full amendment text required by clause 5. No amendment may permit distribution of surpluses or assets to members for private profit, or remove the equivalent restriction on dissolution.

10.2 Amendments must comply with applicable law, restrictions attaching to funds or property and any required regulator consent. The secretary shall maintain the dated current text and amendment record. A change of legal form requires a separately authorised process; it is not achieved by changing the description in this document.

## 11. Dissolution

11.1 A quorate general meeting may resolve to dissolve the Association by a three-quarters majority of votes cast, following notice under clause 5. The resolution shall appoint responsible persons to settle liabilities and identify the recipient of any remaining assets.

11.2 After liabilities and lawful winding-up costs are met, remaining assets shall transfer to one or more nonprofit organisations with similar purposes and equivalent restrictions on private distribution, as selected by the meeting. No assets may be distributed to members. Restricted funds and any property subject to charity law shall be dealt with in accordance with the applicable restrictions and law.

11.3 The responsible persons shall prepare closing accounts, retain the required records and notify relevant banks, funders and authorities. Dissolution does not extinguish personal liabilities or contractual obligations.
